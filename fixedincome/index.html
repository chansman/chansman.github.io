How these numbers are calculated

At auction, Treasury sets the coupon at the winning (high) yield rounded down to the nearest ⅛ of a percent, so a new note sells at or slightly below $100.

Treasury prices are quoted per $100 of face value. A buyer also pays the seller the interest that has built up since the last coupon (accrued interest), and gets it back in the next coupon.

Worth if sold = price/100 × face + accrued interest Accrued interest = (coupon/2 × face) × days since last coupon ÷ days in coupon period Gain or loss = worth if sold + coupons received − paid

The yield to maturity is the rate y that makes the present value of the remaining coupons and the $100 repayment, discounted every six months at y/2, equal to the price plus accrued interest. The current yield is the coupon divided by the price.

How these numbers are calculated

VMBS is a fund that holds agency mortgage pools. It has no maturity date: as homeowners pay down and prepay their mortgages, the fund buys new pools, so its price never converges to a fixed repayment.

Worth if sold = shares × price Gain or loss = worth if sold + distributions received − paid

The 12-month distribution yield is the total paid per share over the last year divided by the price. A distribution counts toward our cash once we own the shares before its ex-dividend date.
